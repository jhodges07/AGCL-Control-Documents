# Point 05.3 — The Enumerated Credit Act

## Purpose

Establish a constitutional budgeting system that enforces strict limits on federal spending, eliminates unauthorized programs, prohibits outsourcing of government authority, and permanently ends deficit-driven governance.

All federal spending must be traceable to enumerated constitutional authority, separated structurally, and enforced automatically.

---

## Core Structure

Federal spending is divided into two distinct and non-interchangeable categories:

* Enumerated Powers Spending (EPS)
* Non-Enumerated Spending (NES)

No blending, cross-funding, or reclassification is permitted.

If authority is not explicitly granted, the spending is classified as NES by definition.

---

## Spending Authority Rules

### Enumerated Powers Spending (EPS)

Spending directly tied to constitutional authority.

* Eligible for limited, structured borrowing
* Subject to repayment schedules
* Must operate within defined authority boundaries

---

### Non-Enumerated Spending (NES)

All spending not explicitly authorized.

Includes:

* Welfare and redistribution programs
* Administrative and regulatory agencies
* Foreign aid and development programs
* Public-private partnerships and indirect governance

NES may not be debt-financed under any condition.

---

## Absolute Prohibitions

### Federal NGO Elimination

No federal funds may be:

* Granted
* Contracted
* Passed through
* Sub-awarded
* Partnered

to any non-government entity.

Government authority must be exercised only by accountable public officials.

---

### Defense and Intelligence Firewall

Federal security agencies are prohibited from:

* Funding non-government actors
* Training or arming proxy groups
* Coordinating through intermediaries

No indirect execution of policy is permitted.

---

## Budget Structure Requirements

All federal budgets must:

* Be organized into defined functional groups
* Separate EPS and NES physically
* Provide independent totals for each category
* Prevent cross-category funding

Spending must be transparent, auditable, and structurally enforced.

---

## Functional Budget Groups

Federal spending is organized into defined categories, including:

* National defense and external security
* Foreign affairs and treaty obligations
* Trade, customs, and border enforcement
* Monetary system and public credit
* Courts and constitutional justice
* Postal and infrastructure systems
* Federal property and territories
* Intellectual property protection
* Welfare and social programs
* Regulatory and administrative state

Each category contains clearly separated EPS and NES components.

---

## Continuing Resolution Elimination

Temporary funding mechanisms are restricted.

* No Continuing Resolution may exceed 30 days
* No CR may fund NES beyond current revenue

If no lawful budget is passed:

* EPS continues at prior levels
* NES funding stops immediately
* All NGO-related funding terminates automatically

---

## Executive Enforcement

Upon budget failure:

* The Executive must reduce or eliminate NES programs
* Priority is given to eliminating welfare, regulatory, and foreign spending
* EPS remains protected and uninterrupted

Enforcement is automatic, not discretionary.

---

## Credit Discipline

* Deficit spending is prohibited
* Total federal spending must decrease annually until debt reaches zero
* Borrowing is allowed only for EPS
* NES borrowing is permanently prohibited

All borrowing must be finite, scheduled, and repaid.

---

## Constitutional Constraint

All federal spending must:

* Be tied to enumerated authority
* Be time-bound and subject to expiration
* Be transparent and auditable

No expansion through interpretation or administrative action is permitted.

---

## Case Illustration

A family of four paying federal taxes:

**Current System**

* Tax dollars fund programs without constitutional limitation
* Spending is blended and difficult to track
* Government borrows to fund welfare and administrative expansion
* NGOs and contractors execute policy without direct accountability

**Under the Enumerated Credit Act**

* All spending is classified and visible
* Tax dollars cannot fund unauthorized programs
* No borrowing occurs for welfare or administrative expansion
* Government authority is exercised only by accountable officials
* Debt declines through enforced limits

---

## Outcome

* Ends unconstitutional spending structurally
* Eliminates deficit-driven governance
* Restores clear limits on federal authority
* Removes indirect and outsourced control mechanisms
* Forces alignment between spending, authority, and accountability

The federal government may spend only what it can enumerate, may not outsource authority, and may not borrow to fund non-authorized programs.
