# AGCL Master Framework

This document defines the structure, sequencing, and governing logic of the
American Gold Contract for Liberty (AGCL).

This is not a policy list.
This is a system.

---

## Purpose

The AGCL establishes a rules-based framework for governance that:

* Restores property rights
* Forces transparency
* Eliminates perpetual taxation structures
* Aligns government incentives with citizen ownership
* Replaces discretionary power with defined systems

## Governing Principle

The AGCL operates under a single structural rule:

Government authority must be:

* Defined
* Limited
* Transparent
* Measurable
* Reversible

If any of these conditions are missing, the system is considered invalid.

---

## Control Integration

All AGCL implementation points are governed by the Control Documents:

* Authority and Expiration
* Signal and Non-Interference
* Property Rights and Pooling
* Exit and Disengagement
* Debt and Intergenerational Liability
* Uncertainty and Stress Controls
* Transparency and Public Ledger
* Constitutional Compatibility
* Enforcement and Remedy
* Amendment and Version Control

Controls override implementation.

If a plan conflicts with a control, the plan must be modified or removed.

---

## Execution Logic

Each implementation point must define:

* Source of authority
* Scope of action
* Duration and expiration
* Enforcement mechanism
* Exit conditions
* Financial structure

No program, tax, or obligation may exist without all six elements.

---

## System Flow

The AGCL is designed to operate sequentially:

1. Replace the federal tax structure
2. Stabilize state and local funding
3. Restore sound money
4. Secure retirement obligations
5. Establish citizen-owned capital systems
6. Enforce constitutional spending limits
7. Restore labor market integrity
8. Eliminate financial surveillance
9. Rebuild judicial balance
10. Eliminate national debt and stabilize long-term systems

Each phase reduces systemic risk while increasing citizen control.

## Final Principle

This framework does not depend on political alignment.

It depends on structure.

A system built on defined rules will outperform one built on discretion.
