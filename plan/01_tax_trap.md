# Point 01 — End the Tax Trap

## Immediate Action Requirement

The first operational step to end the tax trap is the immediate passage of **H.R. 25 — The FairTax Act**.

H.R. 25 replaces federal income and payroll taxes with a single national consumption tax applied at the point of final retail sale. This action begins the transition away from perpetual taxation structures and toward transaction-based taxation aligned with AGCL controls.

---

## Structural Alignment with AGCL Controls

H.R. 25 satisfies multiple core AGCL requirements:

### Authority Shift Toward Transaction-Based Taxation (00C, 00G)

* Eliminates federal claims on income and labor
* Removes tax attachment to earnings and savings
* Moves taxation to visible, point-of-sale transactions

### Signal Visibility (00B, 00G)

* Tax is explicitly shown at the time of purchase
* Reduces embedded and hidden taxation layers
* Improves price signal clarity across the economy

### Partial Exit Mechanism (00D)

* Individuals reduce tax exposure by reducing consumption
* No taxation occurs without a transaction
* Removes compulsory taxation on earnings

### Enforcement Simplification (00I)

* Collection occurs at point-of-sale
* Reduces need for audits, filings, and retrospective enforcement
* Limits enforcement to transaction events

---

## Structural Gaps Relative to AGCL Compliance

H.R. 25 is a transition mechanism, not a complete AGCL-compliant system. The following gaps remain:

### No Explicit Authority Expiration (00A)

* The tax authority created by H.R. 25 does not automatically expire
* Lacks a defined authorization window and mandatory reauthorization

### Exit Is Not Fully Mechanical (00D)

* Individuals cannot formally exit the system entirely
* Tax exposure persists for all consumption within the system
* No contractual opt-out or prepayment exit structure

### Enforcement Is Not Fully Automatic (00I)

* Relies on administrative compliance and retailer reporting
* Lacks automatic invalidation of non-compliant transactions
* Enforcement still contains discretionary elements

### Debt Structure Not Fully Terminated (00E)

* Does not eliminate existing federal debt obligations
* Does not define full extinction timelines for liabilities
* Intergenerational exposure may persist

### Constitutional Constraints Not Structurally Locked (00H)

* Does not fully prevent future expansion or modification of tax authority
* Requires additional constraints to prevent system drift

---

## Required AGCL Completion Layer

To reach full AGCL compliance, the following must be added after passage:

* Mandatory expiration window for all tax authority
* Formal, mechanical exit mechanisms
* Fully automated enforcement rules
* Defined debt termination schedules
* Hard constitutional constraints preventing expansion

---

## Case Illustration

A family of four earning $90,000 annually:

### Current System

* Pays federal income tax continuously with no termination
* Pays payroll taxes funding undefined future liabilities
* Pays embedded taxes hidden in goods and services
* Cannot exit taxation without leaving the workforce

### Under H.R. 25

* Pays no tax on income or payroll
* Pays tax only when purchasing goods and services
* Sees full tax cost at the point of sale
* Reduces exposure by adjusting consumption

### Under Full AGCL Implementation

* All tax authority expires unless reauthorized
* Family can exit tax exposure through defined mechanisms
* No liability persists beyond defined timeframes
* No hidden or embedded taxes exist
* Ownership and income remain fully protected

---

## Outcome

H.R. 25 is the required first step to eliminate the tax trap by removing perpetual claims on income and replacing them with visible, transaction-based taxation.

It advances compliance with AGCL controls but requires additional structural layers to achieve full system integrity.
