I. PURPOSE

This document records the doctrinal foundations and economic lineage that inform the design of the American Gold Contract for Liberty (AGCL).

This document is explanatory only.
It grants no authority.
It creates no powers.
It governs nothing.

All AGCL authority is defined exclusively by Control Documents.
This document exists to explain why those controls are structured as they are.


II. GOVERNING DOCTRINE

AGCL is built on a single governing doctrine:

Reality must discipline authority, not the reverse.

Systems must be designed so that:
• Information flows faster than power
• Errors surface early
• Failure is contained
• Exit is always available
• Time limits authority
• Ownership precedes pooling

AGCL converges on these principles independently of ideology, party, or school.
The economists and thinkers listed below are cited because they arrive at the same structural truths from different directions.


III. CLASSICAL FOUNDATIONS

Adam Smith  
Voluntary exchange, price signals, and decentralized coordination form the baseline for any free system. AGCL’s insistence on visible taxation, voluntary participation, and market-disciplined feedback aligns with Smith’s understanding that coercion destroys signal.

Friedrich Hayek  
Dispersed knowledge cannot be centralized without distortion. AGCL’s Signal and Non-Interference Control, exit mechanics, and prohibition on dark nodes reflect Hayek’s demonstration that planners cannot know what citizens know locally.

Ludwig von Mises  
The economic calculation problem proves that systems without real prices and ownership cannot allocate resources rationally. AGCL’s hard asset anchoring, ownership rules, and rejection of pooled liabilities without property rights directly address this failure mode.

Frank Knight  
True uncertainty cannot be modeled away. AGCL’s Uncertainty and Black-Swan Stress Control formalizes Knight’s distinction between risk and uncertainty by requiring declared failure modes and automatic downgrade rules.

Milton Friedman  
Rules outperform discretion over time. AGCL’s preference for mechanical enforcement, visible taxation, and automatic expiration reflects Friedman’s insistence that discretion invites abuse and instability.

Thomas Sowell  
Tradeoffs are unavoidable and knowledge is constrained. AGCL rejects utopian promises and forces explicit cost, duration, and ownership declarations so tradeoffs are surfaced instead of hidden.


IV. FOUNDING-ERA FOUNDATIONS

Alexander Hamilton  
Hamilton understood public credit as a tool that must be bounded by discipline, time, and ownership. AGCL’s sinking fund mechanisms, debt scoping, and prohibition of perpetual borrowing directly revive Hamilton’s original design logic while removing political discretion.


V. STRUCTURAL CONVERGENCE

AGCL does not adopt economists as authorities.
It observes that independent thinkers converge on the same structural requirements:

• Power must expire
• Debt must be bounded
• Ownership must be explicit
• Exit must be real
• Information must flow unimpeded
• Emergencies must not harden into permanence

Where these requirements are violated, systems drift toward coercion regardless of intent.


VI. RELATIONSHIP TO CONTROL DOCUMENTS

This document is subordinate to all AGCL Control Documents.

No Control Document may be overridden, modified, delayed, or justified by reference to doctrine, theory, philosophy, or economist opinion.

If doctrine conflicts with structure, structure prevails.


VII. DECLARATION

AGCL does not ask citizens to trust economists.
It asks them to trust structure.

The system stands even if every theory is disputed,
because its controls bind authority to reality, time, and ownership.

Doctrine explains.
Controls enforce.
